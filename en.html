The Imagining Body: Aposkopein and the Invisible

Imagination is seeing the invisible. This page compares the poses of 16 figures who “see”, centred on the shading gesture (aposkopein): the hand raised to the brow against the light. Preliminary finding: Pure formulas (satyr, Rembrandt) and witnesses in vision scenes (Brother Leo, shepherds, apostle) have almost identical skeletons — elbow 41–66°, hand above the brow; “what was seen” can only be supplied by iconographic context.

01, the satyr, and 02, Rembrandt’s old man, are formal references. What they see remains unverified. The core study starts with 03–06.

Data and method

The skeleton follows Impett and Moretti (Stanford Literary Lab Pamphlet 16): 12 segments, 11 angles, spine set upright, raised arm mirrored to the left. Four features are added: hand–eye distance (wrist to eye ÷ arm length), elbow angle, upper-arm elevation, and wrist above the eye (in arm lengths). Keypoints were annotated once by one person; images are screen captures; contextual facts are not yet referenced to publications one by one.
