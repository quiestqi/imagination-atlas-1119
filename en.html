The Imagining Body: Poses of Seeing the Invisible

This page takes imagination to mean “seeing the invisible” and looks at how it lands on the bodies of painted figures. The 28 figures come from works in the ICARUS dataset that carry Iconclass codes, supplemented by iconographic subjects: one set is the mental faculties, 52A (52A1 reason, 52A2 foresight, 52A3 memory, 52A4 imagination); the other is the postures of arms and hands, 31A25, and the poses here are described by six of its classes: 31A25341 looking with the hand above the eyes, 31A25311 head held in the hand, 31A2511 arm raised upward, 31A2512 arm stretched forward, 31A2516 hand held in front of the body, 31A2515 arm held downwards. The works range from the 5th century BC to 1913; every skeleton is annotated by hand.

Data and method

The skeleton follows Impett and Moretti (Stanford Literary Lab Pamphlet 16): 12 segments, 11 angles, spine set upright, raised arm mirrored to the left. Four features are added: hand–eye distance (wrist to eye ÷ arm length), elbow angle, upper-arm elevation, and wrist above the eye (in arm lengths). Keypoints were annotated once by one person; images are screen captures; contextual facts are not yet referenced to publications one by one. Image sources are linked from each work; photo 7 by Alvesgaspar, photo 8 by Armin Kleiner (CC BY-SA 4.0); no. 1 is a Metropolitan Museum of Art image.
